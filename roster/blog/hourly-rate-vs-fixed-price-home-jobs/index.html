Hourly rate or fixed price: how to compare home job pricing

Hourly or per-session pricing versus a fixed price for handyman, cleaning and small repairs: what each covers, hidden extras and when each suits.

Two ways companies price the same work

Home service companies usually price small jobs in one of two ways. Some charge by the hour or by the session, so the bill depends on how long the work takes. Others give a fixed price for a defined job, such as assembling a wardrobe or painting a room, whatever the time it takes.

Both can be fair, and both can surprise you if you do not know what sits behind the number. This article explains what each model usually covers, where unexpected costs tend to come from and how to compare an hourly offer with a fixed one. For general advice on lining up quotes, the handyman guide explains how to choose a company.

What each pricing model usually covers

Hourly or per-session pricing

With hourly pricing you pay for the worker's time. It is common for cleaning, where a part-time cleaner is often booked for a set session, and for handyman visits that cover a list of small tasks. The rate normally covers labour only. Materials, cleaning products or parts may be included or charged on top, depending on the company.

Fixed price per job

With a fixed price you pay for an agreed result. It is common for clearly defined jobs such as furniture assembly, mounting a TV or painting a room. The price is set before work starts, based on what you describe, so the company carries the risk if the job takes longer than expected. The catch is that the price only holds for the job as described.

Where surprises come from

Unexpected costs often come from a few familiar sources. Ask about each one before you book.

  • Minimum hours or a minimum charge: some hourly offers have a minimum session length, so a short task costs the same as a longer one. Fixed-price jobs can also carry a minimum job charge.
  • Travel and call-out charges: some companies add a charge for travelling to you or for attending at all, separate from the hourly rate or job price.
  • Materials: paint, fixings, cleaning products and parts may or may not be included. Ask who supplies them and how they are charged.
  • Overruns: on an hourly job, extra time is extra cost. Ask how time is counted, whether it is rounded up and whether the worker will check with you before going past an agreed limit.
  • Scope creep: on a fixed-price job, anything outside the agreed description, such as an extra wall, a damaged surface or a heavier item, can be priced as extra work.
Tip

For hourly work, agree a cap in advance: the worker stops and checks with you before going beyond a set amount of time.

How to compare them fairly

An hourly rate and a fixed price cannot be compared directly. You need to turn both into a likely total for the same job.

  • Describe the job the same way to everyone: what needs doing, the size of the room or item, the wall type and who supplies materials.
  • Ask hourly companies for a time estimate: then work out the likely total, including any minimum session and travel charge.
  • Ask fixed-price companies what the price includes: materials, preparation, clean-up and what counts as extra.
  • Compare totals, not headline rates: a low hourly rate with a long minimum can cost more than a fixed price for a small job.

When each model suits

Hourly or per-session pricing tends to suit routine cleaning, where the work repeats and the time needed is predictable, and handyman visits with a list of small tasks that would each carry a minimum charge if booked separately.

A fixed price tends to suit jobs with a clear outcome: assembling a known item, mounting a TV on a known wall or painting a set number of rooms. You know the cost before work starts, which makes quotes easier to compare.

If a small repair turns out to involve wiring, sockets or the electrical board, stop and use a licensed electrician. Read EMA's guidance on engaging licensed workers to see which electrical work needs a licensed worker.

Questions

Is a fixed price always better than an hourly rate?

No. A fixed price gives certainty for a clearly defined job, but an hourly rate can work out lower for routine cleaning or a list of small tasks. Turn both into a likely total for the same job before comparing.

Why does a short hourly job cost more than I expected?

Usually because of a minimum session length, a travel or call-out charge, or time being rounded up. Ask about all three before you book, and agree a time cap where you can.

Can a fixed price go up after the job starts?

It can if the work goes beyond what was agreed, such as an extra wall or an item that turns out to need more work. Agree the scope in writing and ask the company to check with you before doing anything extra.
