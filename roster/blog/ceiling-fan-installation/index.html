Why a ceiling fan needs a little planning

A ceiling fan looks like a simple fitting, but it combines three things at once: a heavy, moving load hanging from the ceiling, a connection to the fixed wiring, and a control, whether that is a wall switch, a regulator or a remote. Getting any of these wrong can leave you with a wobbling fan, a fan that will not reach the right speed or a second visit you did not plan for.

This article covers what to check before you book, who should carry out the work, how to brief a company and what to ask about removing an old fan. It does not repeat general lighting advice; for that, the light installation guide explains how ceiling light jobs usually work.

What to check before you book

A few minutes of checking at home makes the conversation with a company much shorter, and helps them bring the right parts on the first visit.

  • Ceiling type: is the ceiling bare concrete, or is there a false ceiling made of board below it?
  • Ceiling height: a fan needs enough clearance below the blades for people to walk and reach up safely. Low ceilings may suit a flush-mounted fan, and high ceilings may need an extension rod.
  • Existing point: is there already a ceiling point where you want the fan, such as an old fan hook, a fan point or a light point? A new location usually means new wiring, which the wiring guide covers.
  • Fan weight and size: check the product details for the weight and the blade span. Larger fans with lights attached are heavier, which matters for the mounting.
  • Control: decide whether you want a wall switch, a wall regulator, a remote control or a combination. Some fans with remotes still need a wall switch to cut power fully.
  • Nearby fittings: note any aircon unit, lights, sprinkler or cabinet doors near the spot, since blades need clear space around them.
Tip

Take photos of the ceiling spot, the existing point if there is one, and the wall switch you plan to use. Photos answer most of the questions a company will ask.

Concrete ceilings and false ceilings

On a concrete ceiling, the fan is usually hung from a hook or bracket fixed into the slab. On a false ceiling, the board itself may not be strong enough to carry a moving fan, so the mounting may need to reach the structure above, or a support may need to be added. Ask the company how they plan to mount the fan on your ceiling type before they arrive, not on the day.

Who should install a ceiling fan

Connecting a fan to the fixed wiring, adding a new fan point or changing a switch is electrical work. Read EMA's guidance on engaging licensed workers to understand which work needs a licensed electrical worker. In practice, choose either a licensed electrician or a fan company that uses licensed electricians for the wiring part.

The licensed electrician guide explains how to check a worker's licence and what to ask before the visit.

If you live in a flat, HDB's page on electrical works sets out its guidelines for electrical work in HDB homes. The HDB electrician guide covers how this usually works in practice for flat owners.

Safety

Switch off the power at the DB box before anyone touches an existing fan or ceiling point, and do not try to rewire a fan point yourself. EMA's electrical safety page has general advice on using electricity safely at home.

How to brief the company

A clear brief helps a company tell you whether the job is straightforward and what they need to bring. Send the following in one message.

  • The job: a new fan on an existing point, a replacement for an old fan, or a fan in a new location that needs a new point.
  • The fan: the brand and model if you have bought it already, or the type you are considering. Mention whether it has a light and a remote.
  • The ceiling: concrete or false ceiling, and roughly how high it is, described in words such as "standard height" or "high ceiling".
  • The control: which wall switch or regulator you plan to use, and whether you want it replaced.
  • Photos: the ceiling spot, the existing point, the old fan and the switch.
  • Access: whether a ladder will fit, whether furniture needs moving and any building rules on work hours.

Ask whether the price covers assembly of the fan, mounting, wiring and testing, or only some of these. Some companies supply fans and install them together, while others only install a fan you have bought. Make sure you know which one you are booking.

If the fan has a light kit, ask whether it needs any extra wiring or a separate switch, and whether the company will test both the fan and the light before leaving.

Removing an old fan and mounting the new one

Replacing a fan is often more work than installing on a clean point, because the old fan has to come down first and the existing hook or bracket may not suit the new one.

Questions worth asking before the visit:

  • Removal: is taking down the old fan included, and will they take it away or leave it with you?
  • Hook or bracket: will they reuse the existing hook, or replace it if it is rusty, loose or the wrong type for the new fan?
  • Condition of the point: what happens if they find old or damaged wiring at the ceiling point? Agree in advance that they will tell you before doing extra work.
  • Balancing: will they check that the blades are balanced and the fan runs without wobbling at every speed?
  • Cover and finish: will the canopy sit flush against the ceiling, and will they patch any small marks left by the old fitting?
  • Clean-up: will they clear dust from drilling and take away packaging?
Note

If the fan wobbles after installation, switch it off and ask the company to come back. Do not keep using a fan that shakes noticeably.

Choosing who to call

Often, the decision comes down to whether you already have the fan and how much wiring is involved. If you have bought a fan and there is a working fan point, a fan installer or electrician can usually handle the job in one visit. If you need a new point, a new switch or work on a false ceiling, a licensed electrician who has seen your photos is the safer choice.

Questions

Can I install a ceiling fan on a false ceiling?

Often yes, but the board in a false ceiling may not be strong enough to carry a moving fan by itself. The mounting may need to reach the structure above, or a support has to be added, so ask the company how they plan to mount it before the visit.

Does a ceiling fan need a licensed electrician?

Connecting a fan to the fixed wiring, adding a fan point or changing a switch is electrical work. Read EMA's guidance on engaging licensed workers, and choose a licensed electrician or a company that uses licensed electricians for the wiring.

Should the old fan removal be included in the price?

It depends on the company. Ask whether removal, disposal, a new hook or bracket and balancing are included, and get a written quote that lists each part of the job.
