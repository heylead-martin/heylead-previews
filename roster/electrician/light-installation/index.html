Light installation in Singapore

Install or replace lights in Singapore. Compare replacing a fitting with adding a new point, check ceilings and compare published light installation rates.

By Roster editorialUpdated 12 October 20265 min read

Light installation in Singapore ranges from swapping a ceiling light on an existing point to adding new light points and downlights during a renovation. Replacing a fitting is usually quick and priced per light, while new points involve wiring and take longer. Before booking, say whether you are replacing or adding lights, who supplies the fittings and what the ceiling is made of.

Replacing a light or adding a new point?

The most important question for a quote is whether a light point already exists. Replacing a fitting on an existing point mainly involves disconnecting the old light, mounting the new one and connecting it. Adding a new light point means running wiring from a circuit, adding a switch if needed and making good the ceiling or wall.

Light installation involves electrical work, which must be done by a licensed electrical worker. EMA's guidance on engaging licensed workers explains how to engage one, and the licensed electrician guide explains what to ask.

Supplying your own fittings

Many households buy lights themselves and pay an electrician to install them. If you do, check that the fitting suits your ceiling, has the mounting parts and is rated for Singapore's electrical supply. Have the fittings unpacked and ready before the visit, with any instructions.

Some electricians and lighting shops supply and install fittings as a package. Compare the total cost, and ask about the warranty on the fittings and on the installation separately. If a fitting fails soon after installation, you need to know whether to contact the shop, the manufacturer or the installer.

Ceilings and mounting

The type of ceiling affects how a light is mounted. Concrete ceilings in many HDB flats need drilling and suitable anchors. False ceilings made of board need cut-outs for downlights and may not support heavy fittings without extra support. Tell the electrician what your ceiling is made of, and send a photo of the existing point.

Heavy pendants, chandeliers and fan-light combinations need secure fixing. Ask whether the ceiling can take the weight and how the fitting will be supported. Ceiling fans are a separate job with their own mounting needs; ask about them specifically if you are installing one.

Planning lighting during renovation

During a renovation, plan the number and position of lights before the false ceiling and carpentry go up. It is much easier to run wiring before ceilings are closed. Agree the lighting plan with your contractor and the licensed electrical worker, including switches, dimmers and any smart controls.

In HDB flats, electrical works follow HDB's renovation guidelines; the HDB guidance on electrical works explains the requirements. The HDB electrical work guide covers planning in more detail.

How light installation is priced

Companies usually price light installation per fitting or per point. Replacing a fitting on an existing point is typically cheaper than adding a new point, which involves wiring. Downlights may be priced per piece, with extra for cutting new holes. Heavy or decorative fittings, high ceilings and concealed wiring cost more.

The published rates further down this page come from company websites in the Roster directory, one matching rate per company with its source and capture date. Check each source for whether the price is for replacing or adding a light, whether the fitting is included and any minimum charge.

  • Is the price per fitting or per point?
  • Is the fitting included, or do I supply it?
  • Is there extra for new wiring, cut-outs or high ceilings?
  • Is removal and disposal of old lights included?
  • Is there a minimum charge or transport fee, and is GST included?

When lights flicker or stop working

If a light flickers or stops working, first try a new bulb or tube of the right type. If the problem continues, the fitting, switch or wiring may be at fault, and an electrician should check it. If lights flicker across the home or the power trips, the power trip guide explains what to do.

Do not open a light fitting or switch with the power on, and do not touch scorched or warm fittings. Switch off the circuit at the distribution board and call a licensed electrical worker.

Preparing for the visit

Clear the area under each light so the electrician can set up a ladder. Have the fittings, bulbs and instructions ready, and know which switch controls each light. Tell the electrician about any existing problems, such as a light that flickers or a switch that feels warm.

After the installation, test each light and switch, check that fittings are level and secure, and ask how to change the bulbs. Keep the invoice and the fitting warranty together.

Light installation questions

Does installing a light need a licensed electrician?

Light installation involves electrical work, which must be done by a licensed electrical worker. Check EMA's guidance and ask the company who will carry out the work.

Is it cheaper to replace a light than to add a new one?

Usually. Replacing a fitting on an existing point is simpler than adding a new point, which needs wiring and possibly a switch. Ask the company to price each separately.

Can downlights be installed in a concrete ceiling?

Recessed downlights normally need a false ceiling to sit in. For a concrete ceiling, surface-mounted fittings are common. Ask the electrician which options suit your ceiling.

Should I buy the lights myself?

You can, as long as they suit your ceiling and electrical supply and come with mounting parts. Some companies supply and install as a package. Compare the total cost and warranties.
