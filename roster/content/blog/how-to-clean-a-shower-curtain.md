## Why shower curtains get grubby so fast

A shower curtain spends most of its life wet, warm and folded. In Singapore's humid climate, that makes it an easy place for soap scum, limescale and mould to build up, usually starting along the bottom hem and in the folds. The good news is that most curtains clean up well with a little effort, and a few habits keep them fresh for longer.

This article covers washing fabric and plastic curtains, dealing with mould spots, drying, and when damp in the bathroom points to a bigger problem. For a wider bathroom clean, the [deep cleaning guide](/roster/home-cleaning/deep-cleaning/) explains what a professional job usually covers.

## Check the curtain and the label first

Start by working out what your curtain is made of. Fabric curtains, often polyester or cotton blends, usually have a care label near the top hem. Plastic or vinyl curtains and liners often have no label at all.

- **Fabric curtains:** most can go in a washing machine on a gentle cycle, but check the label for temperature and drying advice.
- **Plastic or vinyl curtains and liners:** some can take a gentle machine cycle with towels, others are better cleaned by hand.
- **Weighted hems or magnets:** these can be damaged by a machine, so hand washing may be safer.

Take the curtain off its hooks or rings before cleaning. Check the hooks too, since they collect soap scum and can leave rust marks.

## Washing a fabric shower curtain

Fabric curtains are the easiest to clean.

- Shake the curtain out over the shower tray to remove loose debris.
- Pre-treat any visible spots with a little mild detergent rubbed in gently.
- Wash on a gentle cycle with your usual detergent, following the temperature on the label.
- Add a couple of old towels to the drum to help scrub the fabric and balance the load.
- Rehang it straight after the cycle rather than leaving it in the drum.

Avoid fabric softener, which can leave a film that traps moisture. If the label allows, a warmer wash helps lift soap residue.

## Washing a plastic curtain or liner

Plastic curtains and liners need a gentler approach, since heat can warp or crack them.

To hand wash, lay the curtain in the empty bathtub or on a clean shower floor. Mix warm water with a little mild detergent, then scrub both sides with a soft brush or sponge, paying attention to the bottom hem and the folds. Rinse thoroughly with the shower head.

If the liner allows machine washing, use a cool, gentle cycle with a couple of towels, and take it out before any spin that could crease it. Never put a plastic curtain in a tumble dryer.

> Tip: A cheap liner behind a fabric curtain takes most of the splashing, so the fabric stays cleaner and needs washing less often.

## Removing mould spots safely

Small black or pink spots are common along the hem. For light marks, scrub with mild detergent and a soft brush, then rinse. Some people use a diluted white vinegar solution or a bathroom mould cleaner on plastic liners. If you use a commercial product, follow its instructions, test it on a small area and keep the bathroom well ventilated.

> Safety: Never mix cleaning products, especially bleach with vinegar, ammonia or other cleaners. Some combinations release harmful fumes. Use one product at a time, rinse thoroughly and keep the window open or the exhaust fan running.

Wear gloves when handling mould cleaners. If spots keep coming back quickly or cover most of the curtain, replacing a plastic liner is often simpler than scrubbing it again. ## Drying and everyday habits

How a curtain dries matters as much as how it is washed. Hang it back on the rail fully spread out, not bunched to one side, so air can reach every fold. Open the bathroom window or run the exhaust fan until it is dry.

A few everyday habits keep mould from returning:

- Spread the curtain out across the rail after every shower.
- Leave the bathroom door and window open when possible.
- Run the exhaust fan during and after showers.
- Squeegee or wipe the shower area so less water sits around the curtain.
- Wash the curtain every few weeks, before marks become stubborn.

If you would rather hand the job over, a regular cleaner can add the curtain to the bathroom routine. The [home cleaning guide](/roster/home-cleaning/guide/) explains how to compare cleaning companies.

## When damp means a leak

Sometimes a musty smell and recurring mould are not about the curtain at all. If you notice damp patches on walls, bubbling paint, water staining on the ceiling below or tiles that stay wet long after a shower, there may be a leak in the pipes or the waterproofing.

For a leak in your own bathroom, the [leak repair guide](/roster/plumbing/leak-repair/) explains how plumbers trace and fix leaks. If water is coming through your ceiling from the flat above, or your neighbour below reports a leak, read [HDB's ceiling-leak guidance](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/sitecore/content/hdbinfoweb/home/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/home-maintenance/home-care-guide/ceiling-leaks) to understand how the process works for flats.

## Questions

### Can I put a shower curtain in the washing machine?

Most fabric curtains can go in on a gentle cycle, following the care label. Some plastic liners can take a cool, gentle cycle with towels, but never put a plastic curtain in a tumble dryer.

### How do I get mould spots off a shower curtain?

Scrub light marks with mild detergent and a soft brush, then rinse. If you use a mould cleaner, follow its instructions, ventilate the room and never mix it with other products.

### Why does my shower curtain keep going mouldy?

Usually because it stays bunched and damp. Spread it out after showers and ventilate the bathroom. If mould keeps returning alongside damp walls or stains, check for a leak.
