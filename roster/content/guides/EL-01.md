Electrician services in Singapore include fault finding, sockets, lighting, circuit protection and wiring. Choose the service around the actual job and confirm the licensed worker responsible. Compare the inspection fee, materials and installation scope before booking, while treating power trips, damaged fittings and other safety concerns as assessment needs rather than DIY projects.

## Which electrical service do you need?

An electrical enquiry should distinguish a planned improvement from a fault. Adding a socket, replacing a light fitting and investigating repeated power trips are different jobs. Tell the company what you want changed or what you have observed without trying to identify an electrical cause yourself.

For planned work, include the number and location of fittings and whether you are supplying the products. For faults, describe the timing, affected rooms and anything that changed before the problem began. Keep the information factual and do not open equipment to collect it.

## Check the licensed worker, not just the business name

Electrical work should be carried out or supervised by a Licensed Electrical Worker, as explained by [EMA](https://www.ema.gov.sg/consumer-information/electricity/engaging-licensed-workers). Ask for the responsible person's name and licence details, then check the official register linked from EMA's guidance.

A company's marketing name, logo or review score does not identify the individual responsible for the installation. Clarify that point before the visit, particularly when a general home-services company coordinates several trades. Ask who will assess the site and who signs off the completed work where relevant.

Roster preserves sourced business details and makes them easier to compare. A listing does not mean Roster has verified every worker's current licence or inspected their work. Use the official source for the credential and the written quote for the agreed job.

## Power trips and immediate safety concerns

A repeated power trip needs an assessment rather than repeated attempts to force the system back on. Stop using affected equipment and arrange qualified help. Keep clear of wet, damaged or exposed electrical fittings and do not open the distribution board or bypass a protective device.

[EMA's electrical-safety guidance](https://www.ema.gov.sg/consumer-information/electricity/electrical-safety) explains household protection and the role of the RCCB. For a fire or immediate emergency, use the appropriate emergency service instead of waiting for replies to a routine quote enquiry.

If you need a company outside normal hours, confirm actual availability, the call-out charge and the arrival estimate directly. Roster is not a dispatch service and does not show live technician capacity. A published 24-hour claim is not confirmation that someone has accepted your job.

## Understand electrician pricing

Electrical prices are meaningful only when the scope is clear. A per-point amount may cover a particular fitting and route, while your job could require additional wiring, access or materials. Ask the company to quote the actual location and intended use rather than applying a generic price to every point.

For fault finding, establish the inspection fee, what the visit includes and whether repair work is quoted separately. Ask whether that fee is payable if you decline further work. For planned installation, clarify the product supply, labour, testing and any finishing work.

Check transport, after-hours charges and GST where applicable. Company profiles show published examples when suitable data is available, but those examples do not replace a site-specific quotation. Compare the total and the exclusions together.

## Lights, fans and sockets: prepare the product details

Supplying product information before the visit helps the company plan the work. Share model numbers, installation instructions and photos of the existing location where safely available. Explain the ceiling or wall finish and whether access is obstructed by cabinetry or furniture.

Ask whether the quote includes removing the old fitting, installing the new one, necessary accessories and disposal. If the product you supply is unsuitable or incomplete, agree how the additional visit or waiting time is handled. Avoid buying a fitting solely because its appearance matches the old one.

For heavier fixtures or changes to the support arrangement, ask the company to explain what assessment is needed. A quotation for electrical connection alone may not include every mounting or finishing task involved in the project.

## HDB, condo and landed-home planning

Property type affects the questions to ask about access and building requirements. For HDB flats, refer to the [electrical-work guidelines](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/renovation/renovation-guidelines/electrical-works) with the responsible worker. Permit requirements and wiring conditions depend on the proposed work and the flat's provision.

Condo residents should obtain management requirements before arranging alterations. For landed homes, explain the areas involved, access and any larger project being planned. In every property, ask the licensed worker to assess the existing installation before promising that an additional appliance can be connected as requested.

Coordinate electrical work with renovation and final cleaning. Agree the route and appearance of visible trunking before installation, and identify who restores affected finishes. A clear plan avoids a completed room needing to be reopened because contractors worked from different assumptions.

## Compare companies and keep a useful job record

A good shortlist combines the right service with clear communication and relevant evidence. Read reviews about similar work, considering the count, source and date alongside the average. Then ask the company how it scopes the job, authorises extras and handles a concern after completion.

Keep the initial brief, written quote and any approved changes together. At handover, ask for an explanation of what was completed and the documents relevant to the work. Retain model information for installed products and the contact process for follow-up.

- Confirm the responsible licensed worker and the booked scope.
- Identify supplied products and any assumptions about the existing installation.
- Agree approval before additional labour or materials are charged.
- Ask about testing, documentation and making good.
- Obtain written workmanship and product warranty terms where offered.

## Electrician questions

### Can a handyman replace a socket for me?

Choose the worker based on the electrical scope and the appropriate licence, not the label used to market the business. Ask who is responsible for the electrical work and check that person's details through EMA's official resources. A general handyman booking should not be treated as automatic authorisation for electrical installation work.

### Is a power trip proof that the whole home needs rewiring?

No. It is a symptom that needs assessment. Tell the company when the trip occurs and what areas are affected, without opening equipment or repeatedly resetting it. Ask for the findings and the reason for any proposed repair or rewiring before approving a larger project.

### Can I get a fixed quote for adding sockets?

A company may provide one once it understands the locations, intended use and existing arrangement. Share a clear brief and ask which items are included. If a site visit is needed, establish its cost and whether the final quotation includes wiring, fittings, testing and restoration of affected finishes.

### Does Roster check availability in real time?

No. Roster lists sourced company information and contact options. Confirm the appointment, attending worker and arrival window directly with the business. A quote-request preview does not dispatch anyone or send your details to electricians, so do not rely on it for urgent attendance.
