Aircon installation in Singapore starts with the rooms you want to cool, the equipment configuration and the practical route for pipework and drainage. Ask for a site-specific proposal with model numbers and a full installation scope. Check building requirements before confirming the job, especially when adding or moving equipment.

## Plan the rooms before choosing the package

An installation brief should describe how the home is used, not just how many indoor units you want. Tell the installer which rooms run together, which are used overnight and whether living spaces are open to adjoining areas. A package name alone cannot establish the right capacity for those conditions.

Include a floor plan if available and point out any renovation changes. Moving a partition or opening a kitchen can change the space being discussed. Ask the installer to explain the proposed configuration room by room so you can compare alternatives on the same basis.

Start with the [aircon guide](/roster/aircon/) if you are weighing maintenance against replacement. If installation is the right project, keep equipment selection and the physical installation scope together throughout the quotation process.

## New installation or replacement?

A replacement quote needs to explain what happens to the existing system and its infrastructure. Ask whether existing pipes, insulation, drainage and electrical connections will be assessed for reuse. Do not assume that changing the units means every old component is automatically replaced or suitable to retain.

A new installation needs agreement on routes and finishes before work starts. Discuss where trunking will be visible, how drainage is arranged and whether the installer needs access through cabinetry or false ceilings. Photograph agreed routes or mark them on a plan if that makes the proposal clearer.

Table: Compare the scope of a new and replacement installation

| Work item | New installation | Replacement installation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment | Confirm all indoor and outdoor models | Confirm compatibility of the proposed system |
| Pipework | Agree new routes and specifications | Identify what is retained and replaced |
| Drainage | Agree the discharge arrangement | Assess the existing route and condition |
| Removal | Usually no old system to remove | Specify dismantling, disposal and access |
| Finishing | Define openings and making good | Include affected finishes around old equipment |

## Check HDB and building requirements early

Building requirements should be checked before the equipment is ordered. For HDB flats, the [installation guidance](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/renovation/renovation-guidelines/air-conditioner-installation-works) sets conditions for location, electrical loading and installer arrangements. Ask the installer to identify the requirements for your particular flat, rather than relying on a general claim that no permit is needed.

HDB specifies a BCA-trained condensing-unit installer and an installation report after the work. Confirm who handles the required documentation. Where electrical work is involved, ask who is responsible for that part of the installation and any testing arrangements.

Condo residents should obtain management rules for access, outdoor equipment and working hours. Landed-home owners should discuss safe access and the proposed position of each outdoor unit. Include management or access costs in the quote when they apply.

## Compare equipment and installation separately

A clear quotation separates the equipment from the work needed to install it. Record indoor and outdoor model numbers, quantities and the rooms served. Ask whether accessories, controllers and any optional functions are included in the quoted equipment package.

Then compare installation details: pipe specifications, insulation, drainage, trunking, electrical work and removal of the old equipment. If one proposal is less expensive, identify which of these items differ. A shorter pipe allowance or a narrower finishing scope can change the practical value of the quote.

The [NEA Energy Label](https://www.nea.gov.sg/our-services/climate-change-energy-efficiency/energy-efficiency/household-sector/the-energy-label) provides model-specific efficiency information. Use it to compare the proposed equipment, while recognising that actual use and the installed arrangement affect your household's experience. Do not treat a label estimate as a promised electricity bill.

## What should the written quotation include?

The written quote should identify the complete agreed job and the conditions that could change it. Ask for exclusions as well as inclusions. If the installer cannot confirm part of the route until access is available, record how that uncertainty will be resolved and priced.

- Equipment model numbers, quantities and the proposed room allocation.
- Pipework, insulation, drainage and trunking specifications or allowances.
- Electrical work and the person responsible for completing it.
- Removal, disposal, access equipment and protection of the home.
- Finishing work, testing, documents and handover demonstration.
- Payment stages, GST treatment and written warranty terms.

Discuss the difference between equipment warranty and installation workmanship cover. They may have different providers, conditions and reporting processes. Ask who you should contact first if a problem appears and retain the relevant registration or invoice documents.

## Coordinate installation with the rest of the home

Installation should fit the order of other work at the property. Share renovation, painting and cleaning dates before agreeing to a visit. If pipe routes need to be concealed within planned finishes, the installer and renovation team need the same drawings and access arrangements.

Ask what must be completed before the team arrives and what needs to remain accessible afterwards. Avoid closing up an area before the responsible contractor has completed the relevant checks. Agree who handles patching or repainting rather than discovering that each contractor assumed the other would do it.

For a tenanted property, confirm the owner's approval and who can make decisions on the day. If the installation is part of a move, allow a practical buffer before final cleaning and furniture placement. The installer should provide the expected sequence for the actual site.

## What to check at handover

Handover is the time to confirm that the agreed equipment and work have been delivered. Ask the installer to demonstrate each indoor unit, explain the controls and show the accessible parts of the completed installation. Compare the installed models with the quotation and retain the documentation.

Request an explanation of cleaning and servicing requirements for the equipment. Note who handles warranty issues and what information they will need. Keep photos of the completed work, especially routes or components that may later become less accessible.

If something differs from the proposal, record it before signing off. A written list of outstanding items is clearer than a verbal promise to return. Use the [servicing directory](/roster/aircon-servicing/) when you are ready to plan ongoing maintenance.

## Aircon installation questions

### Can I reuse the old aircon pipes?

Ask the installer to assess their condition and suitability for the proposed equipment. The answer depends on the actual installation, not simply the fact that the old system worked. The quote should state which components will be reused, what checks support that decision and how reuse affects workmanship cover.

### Does a larger system always cool better?

Choose capacity after an assessment of the rooms and the way the system will be used. A larger package is not a substitute for appropriate sizing and installation. Ask the company to explain the proposed capacity and simultaneous-use assumptions, then compare those assumptions across the quotes you receive.

### How long will the installation take?

Request a schedule based on your property, access and scope. Replacing accessible equipment is a different project from installing new routes during renovation. Ask whether the work takes one visit or several, what interruptions to expect and what must be ready before the team can complete testing and handover.

### Who should arrange electrical changes?

Confirm this with the installer before booking. Electrical installation work should be carried out or supervised by an appropriately licensed worker, as explained by [EMA](https://www.ema.gov.sg/consumer-information/electricity/engaging-licensed-workers). The quotation should identify whether electrical work is included or must be arranged separately.
