A choked drain in Singapore may affect a sink, toilet, floor trap or a wider drainage route. Tell the company which fixtures are affected, whether water is backing up and whether the problem recurs. Ask for the inspection and clearing scope before agreeing to a method or treating an advertised starting rate as the final price.

## Describe the blockage before choosing a service

The most useful first description is what you can observe. Say whether the water drains slowly, remains in the fixture or appears elsewhere when another outlet is used. Explain when it started and whether this has happened before. These details are more helpful than naming a cause you cannot confirm.

Share accessible photos and any previous invoice for the same issue. If a product or tool has already been used, tell the company exactly what it was. Do not keep adding treatments while waiting for advice, particularly when the earlier product is unknown.

The [plumbing directory](/roster/plumbing/) lets you compare companies offering choke clearing. Use this guide to ask for a defined inspection and clearing job, rather than approving a generic drain package without understanding where the work will take place.

## Toilet, sink, floor trap or several fixtures?

The affected fixture helps a company plan the visit, but it does not prove the cause. A single slow sink and water backing up through several outlets can require different investigations. Explain the pattern and let the attending professional assess the actual system.

## What should you do while arranging help?

Avoid adding to an overflowing or backed-up fixture while you arrange an assessment. Keep people away from contaminated water and from any affected electrical area. If the situation presents an immediate danger, seek appropriate emergency assistance rather than waiting for a routine service reply.

Do not mix drain-cleaning chemicals or improvise with tools that could damage the fixture or pipe. Tell the attending company about anything already added so it can plan the work safely. This guide is for arranging a service, not for dismantling or chemically treating a blocked system.

If several homes or common areas are affected, inform the relevant building management. Ask how access to shared areas will be arranged. A private appointment may still be useful, but the company needs to know if the job involves more than your own fixture.

## How do companies choose a clearing method?

The clearing method should follow the assessment of the blockage and access. A company might propose manual clearing, an auger, jetting or further inspection, but those are not automatically interchangeable solutions. Ask what the method is intended to address and what is included in the quoted amount.

A camera inspection, where proposed, should have a clear purpose. Ask whether you will receive images or findings and how those findings will affect the repair recommendation. Paying for an inspection does not necessarily include clearing, replacement or a guarantee that every part of the system can be viewed.

[PUB's guidance on plumbing works](https://www.pub.gov.sg/Professionals/Requirements/Licensed-Plumbers/Plumbing-Works) includes blockage clearing among specified simple tasks. If the proposed job changes to regulated replacement or alteration work, check the licensed person responsible for that expanded scope.

## Understand the quote before clearing begins

A choke-clearing quote should identify the fixture or route, the first-visit charge and the work included. Ask whether the advertised amount is a starting price, a fixed scope or an inspection fee. Clarify the cost if the blockage cannot be cleared using the initially proposed method.

Ask how additional time, equipment, dismantling and making good are priced. If the company recommends a more involved procedure after trying an initial method, request the reason and revised total before approval. Keep a record of the agreed change.

An after-hours visit may have different charges, so confirm them directly. Roster's published rates are captured examples from companies, not a universal price for every blocked drain. Compare the same type of job and the same inclusions across your shortlist.

## What if the same drain keeps choking?

A recurring blockage needs a discussion about the underlying issue, not only another identical visit. Tell the company how long the previous clearing lasted and provide any findings or photographs from that job. Ask what further assessment would help explain the recurrence.

Distinguish clearing the immediate obstruction from repairing a damaged or unsuitable part of the system. The first may restore use temporarily without resolving the reason for repeated trouble. Request a written explanation of the proposed next step and what result it is intended to achieve.

If a company offers a return-visit warranty, read the conditions. Ask whether it covers the same confirmed blockage, any blockage in the home or only a particular part of the work. A precise warranty is more useful than a broad assurance that the problem will never return.

## After the visit: checks and records

At the end of the visit, ask the company to explain what it found and demonstrate the agreed result where appropriate. Record any limitations, such as an inaccessible section that has not been assessed. Keep the receipt with the method used and the part of the system worked on.

Ask for care advice specific to the fixture and the finding. Avoid treating a generic list of household habits as a diagnosis of your blockage. If building management needs to be involved, request the information it will need to understand the issue and arrange the next step.

- Keep photographs or inspection findings provided by the company.
- Note any further work recommended and whether it is urgent.
- Confirm who handles a return visit and its possible charge.
- Do not approve replacement work without a separate, understandable scope.

## Choked drain questions

### Is a blocked floor trap always a simple local problem?

Not necessarily. Describe whether nearby fixtures are affected and when water backs up, then ask the company to assess the system. If common areas or neighbouring homes are involved, inform building management as well. The visible location of the water does not, by itself, establish the cause or responsibility.

### Should I book jetting straight away?

Ask for an assessment before selecting equipment as the solution. The company should explain why a particular method suits the blockage and access, what it costs and what happens if it does not resolve the problem. A more intensive method is not automatically the right starting point for every fixture.

### Can a plumber guarantee that a choke will not return?

Ask what the company can stand behind based on its findings and work. A warranty should define the covered problem, period and exclusions. If the underlying cause has not been established, a blanket promise that no blockage will recur is not a substitute for an appropriate inspection and repair plan.

### What should I send when requesting a quote?

Send the fixture type, symptoms, duration, recurrence history, property type and town. Include clear photos from a safe position and explain any products or tools already used. Ask for the initial visit charge and the process for approving additional work, even if the final repair price needs an inspection.
