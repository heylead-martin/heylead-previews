Toilet repair in Singapore can involve a flushing component, a leak, a blockage or replacement of the toilet itself. Start by describing the symptom and where water appears. Ask whether the quote covers diagnosis, parts and installation, then compare repair and replacement only after the condition and fit have been assessed.

## What is actually wrong with the toilet?

A clear description helps the company bring the right information and avoid quoting the wrong job. Say whether the toilet keeps running, flushes weakly, leaks visibly or backs up. Include the brand or model if it is accessible without dismantling the fixture.

Explain whether the symptom is constant or only happens during use. If you have a recent repair invoice, share the relevant part so the company knows what was changed. Do not assume that a recurring symptom means the same component has failed again.

Use the [plumbing directory](/roster/plumbing/) to compare toilet-service providers. For water backing up because of a suspected blockage, read the [choked-drain guide](/roster/plumbing/choked-drain/) as well, since clearing and component repair have different scopes.

## Common symptoms and useful questions

Toilet symptoms should guide the enquiry without becoming a remote diagnosis. Ask the company what it needs to inspect and whether the parts can be identified before the visit. A photograph of the whole fixture and any accessible label can be more useful than a close-up without context.

Table: Prepare a toilet-repair enquiry

| What you notice | Information to share | Quote question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water keeps running | When it happens and previous repairs | Is the mechanism assessed before parts are quoted? |
| Water around the base | Timing and visible location | Does the quote include finding the leak source? |
| Weak or incomplete flushing | Recent changes and blockage history | Is clearing separate from mechanism repair? |
| Visible crack or damage | Whole-fixture photos | Is replacement needed and what is included? |
| Concealed cistern problem | Access panel and model details | What access or making good may be required? |

Keep the area safe and avoid using a visibly damaged fixture until it has been assessed. Do not dismantle concealed components simply to obtain a photograph for a quote.

## Toilet flush and cistern repairs

A toilet that keeps running, refills on its own, flushes weakly or will not flush at all usually has a problem inside the cistern. Common causes include a worn flush valve seal, a faulty inlet valve, a broken flush button mechanism or a leaking cistern hose. Many of these parts can be replaced without changing the whole toilet.

When you call, say whether water runs into the bowl after flushing, whether the cistern fills slowly or not at all, and whether the button feels loose. Photograph the cistern and the model label if you can lift the lid safely. Ask whether the plumber carries parts for your cistern type, and whether replacing the full flush system is better value than repairing one part.

Several directory plumbers publish rates for cistern hose and flush-system replacement on their own pages. Check those company profiles for the conditions that apply.

## Repair or replace the toilet?

The repair-or-replace decision depends on the condition, parts availability and installation requirements. A company should explain whether the problem is limited to a replaceable component or involves the fixture itself. Ask for the reasoning before approving a complete replacement.

Compare the total cost and practical effect of both options when both are reasonable. A repair quote should identify the part and labour; a replacement quote should include the new fixture, removal, installation and any finishing work. Those figures are more informative than comparing the retail price of a toilet with a repair visit.

If you are renovating soon, tell the company. The right short-term decision may differ from a long-term replacement plan, but temporary work still needs to be appropriate and safe. Request written limitations for any interim solution.

## Check the fit before buying a replacement

A replacement toilet needs to fit the existing arrangement or be part of an agreed alteration. Ask the installer to confirm the relevant dimensions, outlet arrangement, water connection and clearance. A similar-looking product is not proof that it will fit the same location.

Common descriptions include floor-mounted or wall-hung, one-piece or two-piece, and different outlet configurations. You do not need to choose by terminology alone. Ask the company to explain which details matter for your bathroom and record the confirmed model in the quote.

For wall-hung or concealed systems, discuss access and the supporting arrangement. If tiles, boxing or cabinetry must be opened, establish who restores the finish. Keep that work visible in the proposal rather than treating it as an unspecified consequence of replacement.

## Who should carry out the work?

The licensing requirement follows the actual scope. [PUB's plumbing guidance](https://www.pub.gov.sg/Professionals/Requirements/Licensed-Plumbers/Plumbing-Works) distinguishes specified simple cistern-part tasks from regulated sanitary work, including toilet replacement. Confirm the appropriately licensed person when your job requires one.

If an initially small repair turns into replacement or alteration, revisit the booking before work proceeds. Ask who is responsible for the revised scope and any required submissions. The company should be able to explain the difference between the original task and the new proposal.

For condo or rented homes, also confirm access, management requirements and owner approval where relevant. Those arrangements do not replace the need for the right worker, but they can affect when the work can proceed and who can authorise changes.

## What belongs in a toilet installation quote?

A complete quote should name the product and define the work around it. Clarify whether the company supplies the fixture or installs one you provide. If you buy it yourself, ask the installer to confirm suitability first and explain how product and workmanship issues would be handled.

- Exact fixture or replacement-part model, with compatible accessories.
- Removal and disposal of the existing toilet where applicable.
- Labour, connectors and other installation materials.
- Any access, tile, floor or wall finishing work.
- Testing, clean-up and the documentation provided.
- GST treatment, payment terms and written warranty conditions.

Ask what happens if the delivered product is unsuitable or a concealed issue is discovered. Agree an approval process for extra work. A quote should not leave you committed to an unknown amount simply because the old fixture has already been removed.

## Handover, leaks and follow-up

At handover, ask the installer to demonstrate the completed work and explain any use or care instructions. Compare the installed product with the agreed model. Keep the receipt, warranty information and any photos of concealed work that the company provides.

If a leak appears afterwards, report the location, timing and symptoms promptly through the company's stated process. Avoid assuming the water comes from the new component without an assessment. Our [leak-repair guide](/roster/plumbing/leak-repair/) explains how to document the visible evidence.

For a rental handover, keep the repair invoice separate from the [move-out cleaning](/roster/home-cleaning/move-out-cleaning/) receipt. Cleaning and repairing the fixture are different jobs, and clear records make it easier to explain what was completed by each provider.

## Toilet repair questions

### Can a running toilet be repaired without replacement?

It may be possible when the problem is confined to a serviceable component, but the company needs to assess the fixture. Ask it to identify the fault, the proposed part and the repair terms. Do not assume a full replacement is necessary solely because water keeps running after a flush.

### Can I buy a new toilet before calling an installer?

It is better to confirm compatibility and scope first. Share the proposed product details with the installer and ask it to check the dimensions and connections relevant to your bathroom. If you supply the product, agree who handles an unsuitable item, missing components or a product warranty issue.

### Does toilet replacement include disposal?

Only if the quotation includes it. Ask whether removal, transport and disposal of the old fixture are covered, along with packaging and clean-up. Also check whether the job includes restoring surfaces disturbed during installation. These items can make two similar headline installation prices describe different totals.

### What if the cistern is hidden behind a wall?

Tell the company before booking and share the model and access-panel details if known. Ask how the system will be inspected and whether additional access might be needed. The quote should distinguish repairing the mechanism from opening and restoring surrounding finishes, with approval required before extra work proceeds.
