Electrical work in an HDB flat should be planned around the actual installation, the proposed changes and HDB's requirements. Use an appropriately licensed electrical worker to assess the job. Confirm wiring routes, product details, permit checks and testing arrangements in the quotation before adding fittings or committing to a larger renovation scope.

## Plan the electrical job around your flat

A useful HDB electrical brief starts with the work you want, not a package based only on room count. Mark the sockets, lights, fans and appliances you want to add or change on a simple plan. Explain how the rooms will be furnished and used.

Include existing concerns such as repeated trips or damaged fittings, but keep fault assessment separate from the wish list for new work. A renovation quote should not assume that an old concern has been diagnosed simply because new fittings are being installed.

Use the [electrician directory](/roster/electrician/) to compare companies. If the issue is urgent, follow the [urgent electrical guide](/roster/electrician/24-hour/) first rather than treating a renovation enquiry as an immediate repair booking.

## What do HDB's electrical guidelines cover?

HDB's requirements depend on the work and the flat's electrical provision. The [electrical-work guidelines](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/renovation/renovation-guidelines/electrical-works) explain permit conditions, restrictions on embedding wiring and testing arrangements. Ask the responsible worker to check the applicable requirements before confirming the route or scope.

A job not requiring an HDB permit still has conditions to follow. Do not interpret the absence of a permit requirement as permission to ignore installation rules. The quote should identify who handles any necessary application, coordination and documentation.

If a renovation contractor is coordinating the work, ask it to identify the person responsible for the electrical installation. Keep the approved layout and any agreed changes together so the electrician, renovation team and homeowner are all working from the same plan.

## Adding sockets and moving electrical points

Adding or moving a point needs an assessment of its intended use and the existing installation. Tell the worker what you plan to connect and where the fitting should be positioned. A photograph of a preferred location does not establish the suitability of the route or circuit.

Table: Information to prepare for common HDB electrical jobs

| Job | Details to provide | Agree in the quote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Additional sockets | Location and intended appliances | Fittings, route and testing |
| Moving a switch | New position and room layout | Wiring changes and making good |
| New lighting | Product and ceiling details | Removal, installation and accessories |
| Ceiling fan | Model, ceiling and mounting arrangement | Full mounting and electrical scope |
| Larger appliance | Manufacturer specifications | Assessment of the required connection |

Ask whether trunking will be visible and how it will meet existing finishes. Decide the position before cabinetry or furniture blocks access. A small change on paper can become a more involved job once the surrounding work is complete.

## Understand the DB box without opening it

The distribution board is part of the home's electrical installation and should not be opened for DIY investigation. If you are concerned about its condition or repeated trips, describe the symptoms to the worker and request an assessment. Do not bypass or replace protective devices yourself.

The [EMA safety guidance](https://www.ema.gov.sg/consumer-information/electricity/electrical-safety) explains that an RCCB disconnects supply when it detects current leakage and sets out household requirements. Ask a licensed worker if you are unsure about the protection in your own flat.

A trip is a symptom, not proof of a particular fault or a need to replace the entire board. Request the findings and proposed scope before agreeing to parts or larger work. Keep the explanation with the invoice for future reference.

## Lighting, ceiling fans and renovation coordination

Lighting and fan work should be coordinated with the ceiling design and the products being installed. Share model information and the proposed locations before the ceiling is finished. Ask who handles mounting, electrical connection and any access panel or finishing work.

If you supply the products, confirm that the installer has reviewed the relevant details. Agree what happens if accessories are missing or the item is unsuitable for the planned location. Avoid assuming that a quoted installation charge includes resolving every product or structural issue.

Schedule electrical work before the final paint touch-ups and cleaning when possible, based on the contractor's actual sequence. If aircon is also being installed, share the [aircon installation plan](/roster/aircon/installation/) with the responsible trades so routes and access do not conflict.

## Older flats and larger electrical changes

The age of a flat is useful context, but it is not a diagnosis of the wiring. Tell the worker about known renovation history, previous faults and planned appliances. Ask for an assessment of the actual installation before deciding whether partial work or a larger project is appropriate.

If rewiring is proposed, request the reason, the areas included and any limitations of retaining existing sections. Compare the work as a defined project, including access, disruption, testing and making good. The [rewiring guide](/roster/electrician/wiring/) explains those questions in more detail.

For changes associated with higher-load equipment, provide the equipment specifications and let the responsible worker assess the requirements. Do not assume that a new socket or a larger protective device is an appropriate solution to a capacity question.

## Compare quotations and check the handover

A useful HDB electrical quote lists points, fittings, routes and responsibilities clearly. Ask whether prices are for supply and installation or labour only. Include removal, disposal, testing and restoration of affected finishes in the discussion so the total is meaningful.

At handover, ask the worker to explain the completed changes and provide the relevant records. Keep the final layout, product information and warranty terms. If part of the job remains unfinished, record what is outstanding and the agreed next appointment.

- Check that the point quantities match the agreed plan.
- Confirm the visible route and finishing standard before work begins.
- Identify who handles permits, testing and other required coordination.
- Ask for approval before extra work changes the price.
- Retain the invoice and the responsible company's follow-up contact.

## HDB electrical questions

### Does every electrical job need an HDB permit?

No single answer covers every job. HDB's requirements depend on the proposed work and the flat's provision, and conditions can still apply when a permit is not required. Ask the responsible licensed worker to check the current rules for your specific scope before the work is confirmed.

### Can wiring be hidden wherever I want?

Do not assume so. Wiring routes need to comply with the relevant HDB conditions and the installation requirements assessed by the worker. Discuss permitted options and the appearance of any surface route before finalising the design. A visual preference should not override the constraints of the building or installation.

### Should I replace all wiring in an older resale flat?

Ask for an assessment of its condition and your planned use. Age, renovation history and symptoms are relevant information, but they do not establish a universal replacement rule. If a larger project is recommended, request the findings and compare the proposed scope with a clearly explained partial-work alternative where appropriate.

### Who handles a fault in a common corridor?

Report a common-area fault to the Town Council or the relevant building-management contact and keep away from damaged fittings. Do not arrange unauthorised work on shared equipment as if it were a private socket inside your flat. Explain the location clearly so the responsible party can route the report appropriately.
