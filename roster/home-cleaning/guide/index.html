Home cleaning services in Singapore range from regular housekeeping to deep cleaning, upholstery care and move-out cleaning. Choose the service by the tasks you need completed and the condition of the home. Compare a written checklist, the charging basis and exclusions before deciding which company's quote fits your booking.

Which home cleaning service should you book?

The right cleaning service depends on the result you want from the visit. Regular upkeep, a detailed reset and a tenancy handover are different briefs. Tell the company whether the home is occupied, furnished or empty and which rooms need the most attention.

A service name is only a starting point. One company's deep clean may include appliance interiors while another prices them separately. A useful quotation lists the work, the expected team arrangement and anything that will not be attempted. Share photos when the condition is difficult to describe.

What belongs in a standard home cleaning checklist?

A standard clean should have agreed priorities rather than an assumption that every household task is included. List the rooms and the surfaces you expect the cleaner to address. If the booking is time-based, identify which tasks matter most in case the available hours do not cover everything.

For kitchens, distinguish counters and external surfaces from appliance interiors or heavily soiled areas. For bathrooms, describe the fittings and any condition that may need specialist attention. For bedrooms and living areas, specify whether the job includes making beds, moving small items or cleaning under accessible furniture.

Window exteriors, high-level work, heavy furniture movement, laundry and organising possessions should be discussed separately. Do not assume a cleaner will take on work that requires equipment or access beyond the booked scope. A checklist prevents disappointment more effectively than a broad promise of a complete clean.

How are home cleaning prices charged?

Home cleaning prices may be quoted by time, visit, property size or a defined package. Compare the same basis across companies. An hourly rate with a minimum booking cannot be compared directly with a fixed-price deep clean that includes a team and equipment.

Ask whether cleaning materials, tools, transport and GST are included where applicable. Clarify whether the quoted hours mean the duration of the visit or total cleaner-hours across several people. For a recurring plan, check the number of visits, expiry, cancellation and replacement-cleaner arrangements.

Company profiles show published rates where usable information is available. When a price is missing, request a quote using the same task list you send to other companies. The final scope matters more than forcing unlike services into a single price comparison.

HDB, condo and landed homes need different briefs

Property type helps a company plan the visit, but it does not describe the whole job. The number of bathrooms, furniture density, stairs, balconies and accessible windows can matter as much as floor area. Include these details when asking for a quotation.

Condo residents should check entry procedures, parking and management rules affecting the visit. For multi-storey homes, explain which levels are included and whether the team needs to carry equipment between them. In any property, point out surfaces with specific care instructions before cleaning begins.

If renovations are unfinished, say so. Construction residue and active works create a different brief from normal household cleaning. Agree whether the booking should happen after contractors have finished and whether another visit will be needed before you move furniture back in.

Choosing a company and understanding credentials

Choose a cleaning company using its relevant services, clear terms and evidence you can check. Read reviews about the kind of booking you need and pay attention to scope, communication and follow-up. A review about an office contract may be less useful for a one-off clean of an occupied flat.

Licensing claims need context. NEA's cleaning-business guidance lists domestic-premises cleaning among excluded work for which a business providing only those services does not need that licence. Do not treat the absence of an NEA licence as automatic evidence that a domestic cleaner is unsuitable.

Ask the company who provides the service, who supervises the team and how concerns are handled. Roster provides sourced business information; it does not employ the cleaners or certify every company's training, insurance or work quality.

Breakages, special surfaces and the end-of-visit check

Agree how the company handles damage and incomplete work before the visit. Ask for its written terms rather than assuming that a phrase such as satisfaction guarantee covers every situation. If insurance is important to your decision, request the relevant coverage details directly from the business.

Identify delicate finishes, existing damage and items that should not be moved. Put important documents and small valuables away. Give the team any manufacturer care instructions for surfaces or furnishings that need a particular method, and ask it to flag uncertainty before applying a product.

At the end of the visit, compare the completed work with the agreed checklist. Raise missed items while the team is present when possible. Keep the invoice and photographs if a follow-up is needed, along with the company's process and deadline for reporting concerns.

Plan a cleaning visit around your household

A practical booking considers access, people and other work at the property. Tell the company about pets, anyone working from home and rooms that must remain available. Ask how long areas may be unavailable after specialist cleaning rather than assuming everything can be used immediately.

For a spring clean or a move, book against your own deadline and confirm availability directly. Avoid leaving several dependent appointments on the same day without a buffer. Aircon work, mounting jobs and other dusty tasks are usually worth discussing before you finalise the cleaning sequence.

  • Prepare a short priority list and share it before the visit.
  • Confirm who supplies products, tools and any specialist equipment.
  • Clarify entry, keys, parking and the person authorised to approve extras.
  • Agree the inspection process and what documentation you will receive.

Home cleaning questions

Is a cleaner the same as a domestic helper?

A cleaning-service booking is for an agreed visit or schedule of tasks. It is not the same arrangement as employing a live-in domestic helper. Check the company's service terms, who employs or supplies the cleaner and what support is available when the usual person cannot attend.

Should I choose hourly or fixed-price cleaning?

An hourly booking can suit a prioritised task list, while a fixed quote can help define a larger one-off scope. Either format needs clear inclusions. Ask what happens if the work takes longer than expected and whether extra time or additional tasks require your approval first.

Are cleaning products included?

That depends on the company and package. Ask for the equipment and product list before booking, especially if you have delicate surfaces or specific product preferences. If you provide materials, confirm that the cleaner can use them and that they are suitable for the agreed tasks.

Does Roster guarantee the cleaning result?

No. Roster helps you compare companies and contact them directly. The cleaning contract and any service guarantee come from your chosen company. Read its terms, agree the checklist and confirm the follow-up process before booking. Roster does not send requests on your behalf.
