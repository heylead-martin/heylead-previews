Mattress cleaning in Singapore

Mattress cleaning in Singapore usually combines vacuuming, stain treatment and a steam or extraction method, priced per mattress by size. It helps with dust, sweat marks and odours in a humid climate. Before booking, check what the method involves, how long the mattress takes to dry and what the company's price covers for your mattress size.

Why mattresses need cleaning

A mattress collects dust, skin flakes, sweat and spills over time. In Singapore's warm, humid weather, moisture can leave marks and a stale smell, and dust can build up deep in the fabric. Regular vacuuming and a protector help, but many households book a professional clean every so often or before a new baby arrives or a family member with allergies moves in.

A professional clean is not the same as replacing a mattress. It can improve hygiene and appearance, but it cannot restore worn support or remove every old stain. Ask the company what result it expects for your mattress.

Common cleaning methods

Companies describe their methods in different ways. Most combine several steps, so ask exactly what the visit includes rather than relying on a single label.

Check the mattress first

Look for the care label and the manufacturer's instructions before booking. Some mattresses, such as memory foam or latex, can be damaged by too much moisture or heat, and some warranties set conditions for cleaning. Share the brand, type and size with the company, and ask whether its method suits your mattress.

Tell the company about stains and what caused them if you know. Urine, blood, sweat and drink spills need different treatments, and old stains may not come out fully. Mention any odour as well, since the source affects the treatment.

Drying time and timing the clean

Drying matters in a humid climate. A damp mattress can smell musty or grow mould, so ask how much moisture the method leaves behind and how long you should wait before using it. Book the clean in the morning if possible, keep the room ventilated and run a fan or the aircon.

Have spare bedding or another place to sleep ready in case the mattress is not dry by night. Ask the company what to do if it still feels damp after the expected time.

How mattress cleaning is priced

Most companies price mattress cleaning per piece by size, such as single, queen or king, sometimes with a minimum order or a transport charge. Some offer bundles with sofa or curtain cleaning. Cleaning both sides, treating heavy stains and a separate pillow or bed-frame clean can cost extra.

The published rates further down this page come from company websites in the Roster directory, one matching rate per company with its source and capture date. Compare them for the same mattress size, and check the source for minimums, inclusions and GST.

  • Is the price per mattress, and for which size?
  • Are both sides cleaned?
  • Is stain treatment included, or charged per stain?
  • Is there a minimum order or a transport charge?
  • Is GST included?

Allergies and hygiene claims

Some companies describe their service as removing dust mites or allergens. Vacuuming with a fine filter, heat and drying can help reduce them, but results depend on the method, the time spent and the mattress. Treat broad claims with care, and ask the company to explain what it does and how.

If someone at home has asthma or allergies, ask about the products used and whether they leave a scent. Keep the room ventilated during and after the clean.

Keeping a mattress clean between visits

A washable mattress protector is the easiest way to keep stains and sweat away from the mattress. Wash it and the bedding regularly, following the care labels. Vacuum the mattress surface from time to time and let it air with the bedding off when the room is well ventilated.

Rotate the mattress if the manufacturer recommends it, and deal with spills straight away by blotting rather than rubbing. Avoid soaking a mattress with water or strong chemicals at home.

Preparing for the visit

Strip the bed and remove pillows and protectors before the cleaner arrives. Clear space around the bed so the cleaner can reach all sides, and make sure there is a power point nearby. Point out stains and problem areas at the start.

After the clean, check the surface in good light while the cleaner is still there. Ask about drying, and when you can put the protector and bedding back on.

Mattress cleaning questions

How often should a mattress be professionally cleaned?

There is no fixed rule. It depends on use, allergies, pets and whether you use a protector. Many households clean when stains or odours appear, or before a new family member uses the bed. Ask the company for advice based on the mattress's condition.

Can all stains be removed from a mattress?

Not always. Fresh stains respond better than old ones, and some, such as dye or long-set marks, may only lighten. Tell the company what caused each stain and ask what result to expect.

How long after cleaning can I sleep on the mattress?

It depends on the method and how much moisture it leaves. Ask the company for the expected drying time, keep the room ventilated and have spare bedding ready in case the mattress is still damp at night.

Is mattress cleaning safe for memory foam?

Some methods suit memory foam, while too much moisture or heat can damage it. Check the manufacturer's care instructions and ask the company which method it will use for your mattress.
