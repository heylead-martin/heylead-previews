The right aircon type for a Singapore home depends on room layout, available installation space and how many rooms need cooling together. Split and multi-split systems, cassette and ducted units, and portable options have different installation and maintenance needs. Compare those practical requirements before choosing equipment or asking for an installation quote.

## Start with the space you want to cool

Choose an aircon type after describing the space, not before. A compact bedroom, an open living area and a room with limited external installation options present different constraints. Tell the installer how the rooms are used and which ones need cooling at the same time.

Separate the equipment type from its capacity. Two systems in the same category can have different capabilities and installation requirements. Ask for proposed model numbers and a room-by-room explanation so that comparisons are based on your home rather than a generic product label.

The [aircon overview](/aircon/) explains where installation fits alongside servicing and repairs. If you already have a system, ask whether an assessment could help you decide between maintaining it and changing the arrangement.

## Split and multi-split systems

A split system has indoor and outdoor components, while a multi-split arrangement connects several indoor units to an outdoor system. The practical questions are where the components go, how the rooms are connected and what the proposed combination can support when rooms run together.

Package terms such as System 2 or System 3 commonly describe the number of indoor units in the offer. They do not, by themselves, tell you the capacity allocated to each room or the full installation scope. Confirm the exact configuration in the written quotation.

For multi-room homes, discuss both current use and realistic future use. A room used only occasionally may still need to be included in the simultaneous-use discussion. Ask the installer to explain the trade-offs of the proposed arrangement rather than choosing only on the number of indoor units.

## Cassette and ducted aircon

Cassette and ducted arrangements need a different installation conversation from a wall-mounted unit. Discuss ceiling space, access for maintenance and the placement of air outlets. The finished appearance matters, but so does the ability to inspect, clean and repair the equipment later.

A cassette unit is associated with a ceiling installation, while a ducted arrangement distributes air through planned routes and outlets. Ask how the proposed system affects ceiling work, access panels and any renovation already underway. Get the relevant contractors to agree the sequence before finishes are completed.

Do not assume that a service package priced for wall-mounted units covers these arrangements. Tell servicing companies the actual type and model when enquiring. The [servicing guide](/aircon-servicing/guide/) explains why access and equipment type belong in the initial brief.

## Portable and window options

Portable and window aircon options also need to be checked against the room and property. Before buying, establish the ventilation or installation arrangement required by the specific model. A unit's compact appearance does not mean it can be placed anywhere without preparation.

Ask about the practical space occupied by the equipment, any required exhaust arrangement, noise expectations and access to suitable power. If you rent, check the owner's permission for changes to windows or fixtures. Avoid making modifications based only on a product photograph.

Compare the manufacturer's instructions and the appropriate building requirements. If the purpose is temporary cooling while another system is repaired, discuss the expected duration and room use. A short-term purchase decision may be different from choosing permanent equipment for the whole home.

## Compare the options side by side

A useful comparison table highlights installation and upkeep questions, rather than declaring one type right for everyone. Use the options below to prepare a brief for the installer. The company should assess whether your preferred arrangement is suitable for the actual property.

Table: Practical questions for different aircon types

| Type | Planning focus | Maintenance question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single split | Indoor and outdoor positions for the room | Can both components be accessed? |
| Multi-split | Room combination and simultaneous use | How are individual units and shared equipment covered? |
| Cassette | Ceiling space and installation access | What access is required for cleaning? |
| Ducted | Routes, outlets and coordination with renovation | Which ducts and components are in the service scope? |
| Portable | Model-specific exhaust and placement | What owner care does the manual require? |
| Window | Suitable opening and installation requirements | How is safe servicing access arranged? |

Once you narrow the options, ask two companies to quote the same brief. A like-for-like comparison is easier when each proposal lists its assumptions explicitly.

## Efficiency, capacity and the Energy Label

Energy efficiency and cooling capacity answer different questions. Capacity concerns the proposed cooling task; efficiency concerns how the model performs under the relevant test conditions. Ask the installer to address both instead of treating a high efficiency rating as proof that the unit is correctly sized.

The [NEA Energy Label](https://www.nea.gov.sg/our-services/climate-change-energy-efficiency/energy-efficiency/household-sector/the-energy-label) gives model-specific efficiency and estimated energy information. Compare the actual proposed models and read the label assumptions. Household use may differ from those assumptions, so the estimate should not be presented as your guaranteed running cost.

Also compare controls, service access, warranty arrangements and availability of support. These are practical ownership questions that an efficiency label does not answer. Keep the model details with the quote so you can check that the equipment delivered matches the selection.

## Installation constraints and total ownership cost

The cost of an aircon choice includes installing and maintaining it, not just purchasing the equipment. Pipe routes, electrical work, ceiling work, access and disposal can affect the initial total. Servicing requirements and the ease of accessing components matter after installation.

For HDB flats, check the [air-conditioner installation guidance](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/renovation/renovation-guidelines/air-conditioner-installation-works) with the installer. For condos, obtain management requirements before finalising equipment positions. A technically attractive option is not a complete plan until the building constraints have been considered.

Use the [installation guide](/aircon/installation/) for a quote checklist and the [servicing price guide](/aircon/servicing-price/) to understand maintenance charging formats. Ask for the proposed system's own service scope rather than applying a general wall-unit rate to every configuration.

## Aircon type questions

### Is a multi-split system the same as central aircon?

The terms describe different arrangements and are sometimes used loosely in conversation. Ask the company to identify the actual indoor units, outdoor equipment and distribution method in its proposal. A diagram and model list are more useful than relying on a broad label that different suppliers may interpret differently.

### Can I replace only one indoor unit?

Ask the company to check compatibility with the existing system before buying a replacement. The answer depends on the equipment combination and its condition. Provide the indoor and outdoor model numbers if safely accessible, and request written confirmation of the proposed arrangement and any warranty implications.

### Which type is easiest to maintain?

Maintenance depends on the specific equipment, installation access and service scope. Ask how each proposed arrangement will be cleaned and repaired once the room is furnished. A tidy installation should still leave the access the technician needs; concealing equipment without planning access can complicate future work.

### Should I choose the same type in every room?

Base the decision on the rooms and the configuration the installer can support. Different spaces can have different needs, but the overall system still needs to be suitable as a whole. Explain your priorities for appearance, use and maintenance, then ask for the trade-offs of the available arrangements.
